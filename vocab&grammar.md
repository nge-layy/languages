# German Vocabulary & Grammar Reference (A1–B2)

A self-study reference for learners of German, covering core vocabulary organized by topic and the essential grammar you need to use it correctly.

## How to use this document

- Work through one noun category or grammar section at a time.
- Every vocabulary entry has a simple example sentence with an English translation — read it aloud.
- Grammar tables (articles, cases, tenses) are reference material — come back to them often.
- All example sentences use simple A1–A2 German so you can understand them even as a beginner.

# 2. Articles

German has three grammatical genders (masculine, feminine, neuter) and one plural form, each declined across four cases.

## 2.1 Definite Articles (der/die/das)

| Case | Masculine | Feminine | Neuter | Plural |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nominative | der | die | das | die |
| Accusative | den | die | das | die |
| Dative | dem | der | dem | den (+n) |
| Genitive | des (+s/es) | der | des (+s/es) | der |

Example: **der Mann** (nom.) → **den Mann** (acc.) → **dem Mann** (dat.) → **des Mannes** (gen.)

## 2.2 Indefinite Articles (ein/eine)

| Case | Masculine | Feminine | Neuter | Plural |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nominative | ein | eine | ein | — (no plural form) |
| Accusative | einen | eine | ein | — |
| Dative | einem | einer | einem | — |
| Genitive | eines (+s/es) | einer | eines (+s/es) | — |

## 5.2 Verb Type Reference

- **Strong verbs** (starke Verben) change their stem vowel in the Präteritum and often the past participle (e.g., sprechen → sprach → gesprochen).
- **Weak verbs** (schwache Verben) are regular: they add -te in the Präteritum and ge-...-t in the participle (e.g., kaufen → kaufte → gekauft).
- **Mixed verbs** (gemischte Verben) combine a vowel change with the weak -te ending (e.g., denken → dachte → gedacht).
- **Separable verbs** (trennbare Verben) split their prefix to the end of the clause in the present tense (e.g., aufstehen → Ich stehe auf.).
- **Inseparable verbs** (untrennbare Verben) keep prefixes like be-, ge-, ver-, er-, ent- attached at all times (e.g., verstehen → Ich verstehe.).
- **Reflexive verbs** (reflexive Verben) require a reflexive pronoun (e.g., sich freuen → Ich freue mich.).

# 6. German Verb Tenses

## 6.1 Präsens (Present Tense)

**Formation:** stem + personal ending (-e, -st, -t, -en, -t, -en)

**Uses:** current actions, general facts, near-future plans.

| Pronoun | machen (to do) |
|---|---|
| ich | mache |
| du | machst |
| er/sie/es | macht |
| wir | machen |
| ihr | macht |
| sie/Sie | machen |

Example: "Ich **mache** meine Hausaufgaben." (I am doing my homework.)

## 6.2 Perfekt (Present Perfect)

**Formation:** haben/sein (conjugated) + past participle (at the end of the clause)

**Uses:** the standard spoken past tense in German.

Example: "Ich **habe** Pizza **gegessen**." (I ate/have eaten pizza.)
Example: "Sie **ist** nach Hause **gegangen**." (She went/has gone home.)

## 6.3 Präteritum (Simple Past)

**Formation:** stem (+ vowel change for strong verbs) + endings (-, -st, -, -en, -t, -en)

**Uses:** narrative past, mainly in writing and with common verbs like sein/haben in speech.

Example: "Ich **war** müde." (I was tired.) / "Er **ging** nach Hause." (He went home.)

## 6.4 Plusquamperfekt (Past Perfect)

**Formation:** hatte/war (Präteritum of haben/sein) + past participle

**Uses:** an action completed before another past action.

Example: "Ich **hatte** bereits **gegessen**, als er ankam." (I had already eaten when he arrived.)

## 6.5 Futur I (Simple Future)

**Formation:** werden (conjugated) + infinitive

**Uses:** future plans, predictions, assumptions.

Example: "Ich **werde** morgen **kommen**." (I will come tomorrow.)

## 6.6 Futur II (Future Perfect)

**Formation:** werden (conjugated) + past participle + haben/sein

**Uses:** an action that will be completed before a point in the future.

Example: "Ich **werde** das bis morgen **gemacht haben**." (I will have done that by tomorrow.)

# 7. Cases (Kasus)

## 7.1 Nominative (Nominativ)

- **Purpose:** marks the subject of the sentence.
- **Question words:** wer? (who?) / was? (what?)
- **Signal words:** der, die, das, ein, eine
- Example: "**Der Hund** bellt." (The dog barks.)

## 7.2 Accusative (Akkusativ)

- **Purpose:** marks the direct object.
- **Common verbs:** haben, sehen, kaufen, brauchen, essen
- **Prepositions (always accusative):** durch, für, gegen, ohne, um
- Example: "Ich sehe **den Hund**." (I see the dog.)

## 7.3 Dative (Dativ)

- **Purpose:** marks the indirect object (to/for whom).
- **Common verbs:** helfen, danken, gefallen, gehören, geben
- **Prepositions (always dative):** aus, bei, mit, nach, seit, von, zu
- Example: "Ich gebe **dem Hund** Futter." (I give the dog food.)

## 7.4 Genitive (Genitiv)

- **Purpose:** shows possession ("of").
- **Usage:** mostly formal/written German; often replaced by "von + dative" in speech.
- Example: "Das ist das Haus **des Mannes**." (That is the man's house.)

German nouns are masculine (der), feminine (die), or neuter (das). Gender must be memorized with each noun, but these patterns help:

# 9. German Word Order

## 9.1 Main Clauses — Verb-Second (V2) Rule

The conjugated verb is always the **second element** in a main clause — not necessarily the second word.

```
[Element 1] – [Verb] – [rest of sentence]
Ich          trinke   heute Kaffee.
Heute        trinke   ich Kaffee.
```
Both mean "I am drinking coffee today" — only the emphasis changes.

## 9.2 Subject Inversion

When something other than the subject starts the sentence, subject and verb invert:
"**Morgen** komme **ich**." (Tomorrow I am coming.)

## 9.3 Time – Manner – Place (TMP)

Adverbial elements follow this order:
"Ich fahre **morgen** (time) **mit dem Auto** (manner) **nach Berlin** (place)."
(I'm driving to Berlin tomorrow by car.)

## 9.4 Questions

- **Yes/No questions:** verb comes first. "**Kommst** du morgen?" (Are you coming tomorrow?)
- **W-questions:** question word + verb. "**Wann kommst** du?" (When are you coming?)

## 9.5 Commands (Imperative)

Verb comes first: "**Komm** her!" (Come here!) / "**Setzen** Sie sich!" (Please sit down.)

## 9.6 Subordinate Clauses — Verb-Final Rule

In subordinate clauses (after weil, dass, wenn, obwohl, etc.), the conjugated verb moves to the **end**.

"Ich bleibe zu Hause, **weil** es **regnet**." (I'm staying home because it's raining.)

## 9.7 Relative Clauses

Also follow the verb-final rule: "Der Mann, **der** dort **wohnt**, ist nett." (The man who lives there is nice.)

## 9.8 Modal Verbs, Perfect Tense & Future Tense

The conjugated part stays in position 2; the infinitive/participle goes to the end.

- Modal: "Ich **muss** heute **arbeiten**." (I have to work today.)
- Perfect: "Ich **habe** gestern **gearbeitet**." (I worked yesterday.)
- Future: "Ich **werde** morgen **arbeiten**." (I will work tomorrow.)

## 9.9 Negation

`nicht` typically goes at the end of the clause, or directly before the element it negates:
"Ich komme **nicht**." (I'm not coming.) / "Ich komme **nicht heute**." (I'm not coming today.)
`kein` negates nouns (see Section 2.3).

## 12.4 Money, Phone Numbers & Dates

- Money: "Das kostet **zehn Euro fünfzig**." (That costs ten euros fifty.)
- Phone numbers are read digit by digit: "null-eins-fünf-zwei..." 
- Dates: "Heute ist der **achte August**." (Today is the eighth of August.)
- Years: "Ich bin **neunzehnhundertneunzig** geboren." (I was born in 1990.)

# 15. Grammar Tips

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Article mistakes:** German gender doesn't always match English logic (das Mädchen is neuter, not feminine, because -chen is a diminutive ending). Always learn the article with the noun.

**Case mistakes:** Remember that prepositions like "mit," "nach," "von" always take dative, while "für," "durch," "ohne" always take accusative — these must simply be memorized.

**Word order mistakes:** In subordinate clauses (after weil, dass, wenn), the verb goes to the end — a very common mistake for English speakers is keeping the verb in second position.

**Verb mistakes:** Be careful with separable verbs — "aufstehen" becomes "Ich stehe auf" in the present tense, not "Ich aufstehe."

**Plural mistakes:** German plurals are irregular and must be memorized with each noun (der Mann → die Männer, das Kind → die Kinder, die Frau → die Frauen).

**Gender mistakes:** Days, months, and seasons are almost always masculine (der Montag, der Winter), while most nouns ending in -ung, -heit, -keit are feminine.
